what is papergolf?

papergolf is a minimalist golf game that you can play on paper. it is designed to be printed and played with a pencil, but you can also play it digitally.

it was created by Tom Brinton, for Paper Apps. (please buy his game)

this game is way better played on paper than on a screen, but this port still captures the essence of the original, and adds some new features.

how to play

the goal is simple:

i think it's already obvious enough, but just in case, your job is to get the ball into the hole.

every course has six randomly generated holes. finish all six to complete the course.

rolling & moving

each turn, roll a d6 to determine how many spaces you'll advance. you can move in any of the 8 directions: up, down, left, right, or any diagonal.

if you're positioned on the fairway, add 1 to your roll. if you're in a sand trap, subtract 1. you can also choose to putt and move just 1 space instead of rolling.

fairway 		+1 to your roll and putt, can fly over trees
rough 		default terrain, no special effects
sand 		-1 to your roll

path & obstacles

before committing, check that your route is clear. water hazards and trees block your path completely — you can't land on them. the only exception: trees can be cleared if you're teeing off from the fairway.

you're always allowed to head in the opposite direction of your previous shot if needed.

mulligans

don't like your roll when teeing off? re-roll once and stick with the new result. you also get 6 mulligans per course — these let you re-roll at any point during play.

scoring

count every stroke you take. the par for each hole is 6. go under, feel great. go over, no big deal — it's just paper golf.

terrain

rough: the default terrain. no special effects on your roll.

fairway: gives you a +1 bonus to your roll and lets you fly over trees.

sand: costs you a -1 penalty on your roll.

water: completely off-limits. you can fly over it, but never land in it.

trees: also off-limits for landing or passing through, unless you're coming from the fairway.

slopes: marked with arrows. if you land on one, your ball rolls 1 extra space in that direction. if that space also has a slope, it keeps going until it stops on a neutral tile.
